## Executive Summary

BTCI is best understood as a Bitcoin exposure product with a systematic option-income overlay, not as a high-yield substitute for cash. The fund seeks monthly income from a data-driven call-option strategy while obtaining Bitcoin exposure through exchange-traded products. That structure can produce substantial distributions when Bitcoin volatility is high, but it also exchanges part of the upside for current cash and does little to protect the portfolio during a sustained decline.

Our analysis uses data available through 2026-09-02. In the observed Bitcoin drawdown from the 2025-10-06 peak to the 2026-06-30 low, Bitcoin fell 53.1% while BTCI fell 48.4%. That means BTCI captured approximately 91% of the underlying decline. The option premium reduced the loss, but it did not turn the strategy into a defensive allocation. Since inception, our price and distribution comparison also shows the central trade-off: BTCI's estimated total return was 10.1% with distributions reinvested, versus 14.6% for the comparison Bitcoin exposure over the same analysis window.

## Strategy and Business Model

NEOS launched BTCI on 2024-10-17. The fund invests in exchange-traded products with direct Bitcoin exposure and sells out-of-the-money calls to generate option premium. The issuer reports a monthly distribution schedule and a 0.98% management fee in its fact sheet. The call overlay leaves investors exposed to Bitcoin's price risk while reducing participation in a sharp rally. In practical terms, BTCI is a trade between three outcomes: Bitcoin exposure, periodic cash distributions, and capped or reduced upside participation.

This distinction matters because a distribution rate is not the same as an investment return. A payment can come from option premium, dividends, capital gains, interest, or return of capital, and the mix can change over time. When the net asset value declines, a high distribution can coexist with a shrinking capital base. The relevant comparison is therefore total return after distributions, not the annualized distribution rate displayed at a single point in time.

## Evidence

The recent price series illustrates the volatility of the product. BTCI traded between approximately $26.86 and $33.09 in the 50-trading-day chart ending 2026-09-02. It rebounded 19.1% from the observed low to $32.00, but that rebound does not erase the earlier drawdown. The fund's monthly distribution also varied materially: the observed single-payment range was about $0.629 to $1.572, with a coefficient of variation of 28.2% in the available sample.

The downside and upside beta split is more informative than the headline yield. BTCI's estimated downside beta was 0.83 and its upside beta was 0.75 in our comparison. The 0.08 difference suggests that the observed reduction in downside participation was small relative to the upside participation given away. During the Bitcoin drawdown, the monthly payment fell from approximately $1.30 in October 2025 to $0.653 near the June 2026 low, a 49.8% decline that broadly tracked the reduction in net asset value. Payments then remained near recent lows even as the price partially recovered.

The distribution classification also needs careful interpretation. NEOS states that BTCI distributions may include option premiums, dividends, capital gains, interest, and return of capital. Return of capital may be tax-efficient in some jurisdictions, but it is not automatically economic profit: it can reduce an investor's cost basis. U.S. withholding, local tax treatment, and reporting obligations are separate questions that depend on the investor's tax residence, status, and applicable rules. The issuer's 19a-1 notices and annual tax forms should take precedence over any estimate in this report, and investors should obtain advice appropriate to their own jurisdiction.

## Key Risks

- Bitcoin remains a highly volatile underlying asset. The option premium is not a floor under the NAV, and a covered-call overlay can still capture most of a prolonged decline.
- The strategy gives up some upside in exchange for current income. It is not equivalent to holding spot Bitcoin without an option overlay.
- A high distribution can include return of capital. Cash received is not proof that wealth increased, especially when the NAV and price return are negative.
- Bitcoin exposure is obtained through ETPs, so investors also face custody, counterparty, liquidity, regulatory, and product-structure risks that differ from a conventional equity ETF.
- BTCI began in 2024 and has not yet produced evidence across multiple complete Bitcoin market cycles. Estimates from the current sample should not be treated as a forecast.

## Analyst Conclusion

BTCI may suit an investor who specifically wants Bitcoin exposure with a variable monthly cash-flow mechanism and accepts that the income can fall sharply in a bear market. The evidence through 2026-09-02 does not support treating its distribution rate as a substitute for a stable yield or as downside protection. BTCI captured approximately 91% of the observed Bitcoin decline, its upside beta was lower than its downside beta, and its estimated total return lagged the direct Bitcoin comparison despite substantial cash distributions.

The investment question is therefore not whether BTCI pays income. It does. The more important question is whether the investor values that cash-flow timing more than uncapped upside and a larger capital base. If the primary thesis is long-term Bitcoin price appreciation, direct exposure produced the stronger result in this analysis period. If the primary thesis is receiving cash from a volatile Bitcoin allocation, BTCI is a more direct match, but the plan should assume variable payments, NAV erosion, and no guaranteed recovery time. The conclusion would need to be revisited if future bear-market data showed materially lower downside capture without a comparable loss of upside.

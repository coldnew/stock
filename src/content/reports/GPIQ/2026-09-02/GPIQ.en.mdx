## Executive Summary

GPIQ combines Nasdaq-100 equity exposure with a systematic call-option overlay designed to generate monthly income. The fund is an important counterexample to the assumption that every covered-call income ETF must steadily erode its net asset value. Through 2026-09-02, our analysis found approximately 44.0% price return since inception and 94.0% estimated total return after distributions. That record is materially stronger than the NAV paths of several comparable income products in this report series.

The qualification is just as important as the headline result. GPIQ launched on 2023-10-24 and has not experienced a prolonged, deep Nasdaq-100 bear market. Its only major observed stress event in the available record was the April 2025 tariff-related correction, which lasted about 48 days and recovered relatively quickly. A strong NAV during a favorable market regime is evidence of a successful historical period, not proof that the option overlay will provide superior downside protection in the next extended decline.

## Strategy and Business Model

Goldman Sachs Asset Management describes GPIQ as seeking current income while maintaining prospects for capital appreciation. The fund provides exposure to the Nasdaq-100 and dynamically sells call options. Its reported management fee in the analysis data was 0.29%, and the fund pays distributions monthly. Selling calls can convert part of the index's volatility into option premium, but it also creates an opportunity cost when the index rallies beyond the option strikes.

That trade-off makes total return the correct primary measure. The distribution rate can change with volatility, option positioning, and the fund's capital base. A high payment does not automatically mean that the underlying portfolio is generating an equivalent amount of new wealth. Conversely, a distribution does not prove NAV erosion: if the option and equity returns are sufficient, the NAV can grow while the fund pays cash.

## Evidence

GPIQ's historical record through the stated date is unusually strong for this category. The estimated price return was +44.0%, or about 13.7% annualized, while the estimated total return including distributions was +94.0%, or about 26.2% annualized. The monthly payments in the recent six-month sample ranged from approximately $0.43 to $0.52. Measured against the original investment cost, the estimated annualized distribution rate increased from 11.6% at inception to 15.4% in the current analysis.

The comparison with other Nasdaq-linked income funds is more informative than the absolute numbers. GPIQ's estimated upside and downside betas versus QQQ were both 0.896, suggesting broadly symmetric participation in the available sample. QQQI's corresponding estimates were 0.848 on up days and 0.866 on down days. The difference is small, but over many periods even modest asymmetry can compound. GPIQ also had an estimated asset base of about $5.58 billion in the source data, providing a larger operating scale than some newer peers.

The April 2025 correction offers a useful but limited stress test. Volatility rose quickly, and the subsequent rebound helped the option-income mechanism. This type of sharp, V-shaped correction can be friendlier to an option strategy than a slow, persistent decline. GPIQ has therefore demonstrated resilience in one correction, not resilience across a complete bear-market cycle.

## Key Risks

- GPIQ has not yet been tested by a prolonged Nasdaq-100 bear market. Current NAV growth may reflect the strong underlying market as much as the design of the overlay.
- Selling calls reduces participation in a sharp rally. A stable distribution can come with a meaningful opportunity cost versus holding the Nasdaq-100 without an option overlay.
- A reported distribution may include option premium, dividends, capital gains, or return of capital. Final tax characterization should come from the issuer's notices and annual tax documents.
- A larger asset base may improve trading scale, but it does not remove equity-market, derivative, liquidity, counterparty, or tracking risks.
- Historical beta and return estimates depend on dates, benchmarks, treatment of distributions, and the selected market-data sample. They are not forecasts.

## Analyst Conclusion

GPIQ currently has the strongest observed balance of NAV growth and cash distribution among the covered-call income funds compared in this series. Its 0.29% fee, approximately $5.58 billion asset base, and +94.0% estimated total return since inception are meaningful historical positives. The data also undermine the simplistic claim that an option-income ETF must fund every distribution by consuming principal.

However, GPIQ should not be marketed as a proven bear-market hedge. Its only major stress observation was a short correction, not a slow and deep decline. The estimated downside beta of 0.896 was slightly higher than QQQI's 0.866, so the available evidence does not show superior downside protection. GPIQ is most defensible as a Nasdaq-100 allocation for an investor who values current cash flow and accepts reduced upside participation. The thesis would be weakened if a future prolonged bear market showed that distributions fell sharply while NAV captured nearly all of the index decline.

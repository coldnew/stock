## Executive Summary

IQQ should be evaluated by the diversification it actually provides, including country, sector, currency, and valuation exposures.

## Strategy and Business Model

IQQ is an international equity allocation. Its outcome is a combination of country, sector, currency, valuation and index-construction exposures, so the label alone does not establish diversification.

## Evidence

Evidence should compare country and sector overlap, tracking difference, currency exposure, costs and total return with the investor’s existing U.S. equity holdings.

## Key Risks

- Foreign currencies can amplify or offset local-market returns.
- Political, tax, governance and liquidity risks differ by country.
- Market data and classifications change; verify current official disclosures.

## Analyst Conclusion

IQQ is useful only when it adds intentional geographic exposure rather than duplicating existing holdings. The thesis fails when costs, overlap or currency risk outweigh the diversification benefit.

## Executive Summary

JEPQ is the Nasdaq-100-oriented sibling of JEPI. It combines an actively managed equity portfolio with equity-linked notes to generate monthly income, while retaining meaningful exposure to large-cap technology companies.

The central trade-off is straightforward: option-linked income can reduce the cost of holding a volatile equity portfolio, but it can also limit upside participation. JEPQ's headline yield should therefore be evaluated together with total return, drawdown behavior, and the composition of its distributions.

## Strategy Overview

JEPQ uses active stock selection and equity-linked notes to produce income. This structure differs from a simple index fund and differs from products that directly sell index call options. The difference matters because portfolio construction, option exposure, volatility, and counterparty arrangements can all affect results.

## Distribution and Tax Context

Monthly distributions are not the same as economic return. Their composition can include realized gains, option income, and return of capital. Investors should verify the final tax classification in official fund documents rather than infer it from a single distribution notice.

## Evidence and Comparisons

JEPQ's beta is lower than several newer Nasdaq-100 income products in this comparison set, but the available history does not establish that the lower beta will persist during a deep bear market. Comparisons should use the same measurement window, benchmark, and total-return definition.

## Key Risks

- JEPQ was launched after the peak of the 2022 Nasdaq bear market and has not provided a complete live test of that drawdown.
- Option-linked income can reduce upside participation during strong rallies.
- Equity-linked notes introduce counterparty and valuation considerations beyond direct stock ownership.
- Distribution composition can change and must be checked against official fund disclosures.
- A high distribution rate is not a guarantee of future income or total return.

## Analyst Conclusion

JEPQ may suit readers seeking monthly cash flow alongside substantial Nasdaq-100 exposure, provided they accept equity volatility and the opportunity cost of an option-income strategy. The strongest conclusion supported by current evidence is about its structure and observed history, not about its future bear-market protection.

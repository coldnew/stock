## Executive Summary

QQQI is a Nasdaq-100 income ETF that combines equity exposure with a data-driven call-option strategy. It seeks high monthly income, tax-efficient distributions, and some potential for equity appreciation. The central trade-off is straightforward: investors receive current cash flow, but the option overlay can reduce participation in a fast rally and may not provide meaningful protection in a prolonged decline.

The observed record through 2026-09-02 is constructive on cash-flow stability but incomplete on downside risk. QQQI launched in January 2024 and has not lived through a full Nasdaq-100 bear market. In the April 2025 tariff-related correction, QQQ fell approximately 22.8% and QQQI fell approximately 20.0%, an estimated 88% downside capture. That was a sharp correction and rebound, not a long bear market. It should be treated as one observation rather than proof that QQQI will be defensive in a future drawdown.

## Strategy and Business Model

NEOS states that QQQI invests in Nasdaq-100 constituents and implements a data-driven call-option strategy. The fund may sell and purchase Nasdaq-100 index options, allowing the manager to seek both option income and some upside participation. NEOS also highlights the use of NDX index options classified as Section 1256 contracts and possible tax-loss harvesting opportunities. These features describe the intended structure; they do not guarantee a particular distribution, tax result, or total return.

QQQI charges a 0.68% management fee and distributes monthly. A monthly payment is useful for cash-flow planning, but its amount depends on the fund's NAV, option premiums, portfolio results, and distribution policy. The annualized distribution rate can look stable or even rise when the share price falls because the denominator is smaller. Measuring actual dollars received per share and total return provides a more reliable assessment than yield alone.

## Evidence

The available distribution history is one of QQQI's strongest observed features. Individual payments in the sample ranged from approximately $0.531 to $0.659, with a coefficient of variation of about 4.1%, lower than the comparable BTCI sample. Measured against the original investment cost, the estimated annualized cash flow increased from roughly 14.3% at inception to 15.7% at the analysis date. This is evidence of a relatively stable early cash-flow record, not evidence that the pattern will survive a multi-month bear market.

QQQI's 2025 full-year total return was approximately 18.6%, compared with 20.8% for QQQ. During the faster 2026 rebound period, the estimated annualized returns were about 15.5% for QQQI and 24.0% for QQQ. The widening gap is consistent with covered-call opportunity cost: the faster the underlying index rises, the more valuable the upside given away through calls can become. The exact gap will vary with option strikes, timing, volatility, and the treatment of distributions.

QQQI's price return since inception was approximately +8.3% in the analysis, far below the estimated +66.4% price return of the QQQ comparison. Including distributions and reinvestment, QQQI's estimated total return was +56.6%. The fund therefore had not shown absolute price erosion in the selected period, but its NAV growth was substantially diluted relative to holding the underlying index. Cumulative distributions were approximately $19.27 per share, or about 39% of the starting price. That cash flow is economically meaningful, but it should not be confused with a free additional return.

## Bear-Market Scenarios

QQQI has no direct evidence from a prolonged Nasdaq-100 bear market, so the following is a scenario range rather than a forecast. Using the observed QQQI downside beta range of approximately 0.83 to 0.91 as an analytical reference, a 30% decline in QQQ would imply a rough QQQI decline of 25% to 27%; a 40% QQQ decline would imply approximately 33% to 36%. The result could be worse or better depending on volatility, option positioning, and the path of the market.

Recovery time is path-dependent. A fast correction can generate higher option premium and rebound quickly, while a slow decline can reduce the NAV base and monthly dollars paid per share for many months. Based on the BTCI stress example and QQQI's shorter correction history, a hypothetical 30% to 40% Nasdaq-100 decline could require roughly 6 to 10 months to recover in a sharp correction, or 10 to 15 months or more in a slow decline. These ranges are scenario estimates, not promises or timing signals.

## Key Risks

- QQQI has not experienced a complete Nasdaq-100 bear market. Its early distribution stability may not persist when the underlying index declines for several months.
- Call options can cap or reduce upside participation. A high distribution rate may coexist with significant underperformance versus QQQ during a strong rally.
- Distributions may include option premiums, dividends, capital gains, interest, and return of capital. ROC can reduce cost basis and is not automatically economic profit.
- The fund has equity, derivatives, volatility, liquidity, counterparty, tracking, and implementation risks. NDX option tax treatment does not determine an investor's complete tax outcome.
- Scenario ranges based on BTCI or daily beta are not QQQI observations. They should not be used as a price target or a personalized allocation rule.

## Analyst Conclusion

QQQI is a cash-flow-oriented Nasdaq-100 allocation with a comparatively stable early distribution record, not a proven bear-market hedge. The fund's monthly payments varied less than BTCI's in the available sample, but the price and total-return comparison shows the cost of the income overlay: QQQI captured much less of the underlying index's price appreciation. The 2025 correction demonstrated that the fund can fall nearly as much as QQQ during a meaningful shock even when the payment remains relatively steady.

The most defensible use of QQQI is therefore conditional. It may be relevant for an investor who values monthly cash flow, accepts reduced upside, and can tolerate the possibility that both NAV and per-share distributions decline in a prolonged bear market. It should not be selected on the assumption that an advertised annualized yield is fixed, or that the 2025 correction represents the maximum possible loss. Future evidence would improve the thesis if QQQI showed materially lower downside capture through a full bear cycle without an equivalent increase in NAV dilution.
